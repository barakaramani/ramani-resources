Ways to pay your supplier, compared

Six ways Tanzanian importers pay suppliers abroad, side by side: how fast, what it costs, what protects you and what proof you keep for TRA and lenders.

Protection if something goes wrong: more green dots means more protection.

Bank transfer (TT) from your business account

How it works
Your bank sends dollars or yuan to the supplier's bank through SWIFT.
Speed
1 to 3 working days
Cost
A bank fee plus the bank's exchange rate margin. Ask for both before you pay.
If something goes wrong
Hard to reverse once sent, so check the supplier first. The SWIFT receipt lets your bank trace the payment.
Proof for TRA and lenders
Strong. SWIFT proof matches your customs value and helps when you apply for finance.
Best for
Most orders, once you trust the supplier.

Letter of credit (LC)

How it works
Your bank promises to pay the supplier only when the agreed shipping documents arrive.
Speed
1 to 2 weeks to set up
Cost
The highest: opening and handling fees, and the bank may ask for security.
If something goes wrong
Strong. Money is released only against the documents you agreed.
Proof for TRA and lenders
Strong.
Best for
Large orders and new suppliers.

Online platform protection, such as Alibaba Trade Assurance

How it works
You order and pay through the platform, which has a claim process if the order is late or not as agreed.
Speed
Same day to 2 days
Cost
Card or transfer fees and the platform's exchange rate.
If something goes wrong
Good for orders placed and paid on the platform, within its claim rules and time limits.
Proof for TRA and lenders
Good. Keep the order and payment records.
Best for
First orders and smaller orders from online suppliers.

Mobile money and international transfer apps

How it works
Licensed apps and cards send money abroad from your phone or account.
Speed
Minutes to 2 days
Cost
A transfer fee plus an exchange rate margin, with limits per transfer.
If something goes wrong
Depends on the app. Check that it is licensed by the Bank of Tanzania.
Proof for TRA and lenders
Medium. Keep the app receipt with the supplier's invoice.
Best for
Samples and small orders.

Payment agents (wakala wa malipo)

How it works
You give cash or mobile money to an agent in Tanzania and their partner pays your supplier abroad.
Speed
Often the same day
Cost
The agent's charge, often hidden in the exchange rate.
If something goes wrong
Weak. If money is lost there is little you can do to recover it.
Proof for TRA and lenders
Weak. There is no bank record of the payment for customs or lenders.
Best for
Use with care. Get a written receipt and ask the supplier to confirm they were paid.

Import finance from Ramani

How it works
Ramani pays your supplier for you by bank transfer and you repay as you sell, subject to assessment.
Speed
After your assessment and approval
Cost
A finance fee that you see before you agree.
If something goes wrong
The supplier is paid by bank transfer, with a full paper trail.
Proof for TRA and lenders
Strong, and it builds your track record.
Best for
Growing importers who need cash to order more.

Costs and limits change and differ by bank and provider. Ask for the full cost in writing before you pay. This guide is general information, not financial advice.

Six checks before you pay any supplier

  1. Confirm new bank details by phone or video call with someone you know at the supplier. Never trust an email alone.
  2. Check that the account name matches the company on the invoice and contract.
  3. Pay a deposit first and the balance after you see photos or an inspection report.
  4. Ask your bank for the full cost: fee, exchange rate and any charges taken by other banks on the way.
  5. Keep the SWIFT receipt (MT103), invoice and contract together. Your clearing agent and any lender will ask for them.
  6. Watch for pressure. A supplier who rushes you to pay today, or changes the account at the last minute, is a warning sign.
